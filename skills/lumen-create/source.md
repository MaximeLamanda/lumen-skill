# From a drawing the person already has

`read.mjs` wrote `lumen-<name>-source.png`, the drawing as it looks (on dark when its ink is light), and, for an svg, `lumen-<name>-source.txt`, the card: its viewBox, title, counts, the ink's box, and one line per painted shape with its box, centre, paint and the shape it sits in. For a png, jpg or webp there is no card: the picture is the file itself and the proportions are yours to estimate by eye. Look at the picture. Read the card. Then answer these, in order, before any concept is written.

**The drawing gives the identity, not the paths and not the colours. The piece is rebuilt from the kit's primitives, with the source's proportions, in one ink. Never trace a path and never carry over a colour.** The person will know their object by its parts and its proportions, not by its coordinates or its palette.

1. **What is the object?** One word, from the picture, before reading the card. Then check it against the `title`, the ids and the card. When the picture and the card disagree, the picture wins: ids are what the author typed, the picture is what they drew.
2. **Which parts make it that object?** Two or three, each pointing at card lines: `#1 the body, #2 and #3 the fins, #5 the flame`. This is step 3 of `concepts.md`, "name the parts", now with evidence. Keep the list: the piece draws from it, and the look (`look.md`, item 13) checks against it.
3. **Which proportions must survive?** The ink box's ratio, and the two or three relations between parts that weigh most: the fins at the base, each as wide as a third of the body; the flame below, a third of the body's height. These are the numbers that go into the primitives. Everything else is yours to decide in the kit's terms.
4. **What is decoration?** Shadows, highlights, outlines, small shapes added for colour, a second or third hue. It stays out. Colour does the work of depth in the source; in the piece, the fade does it.
5. **Where does the ink gather?** The source's most saturated or darkest part is a good first guess for the core (rule 04), but not a rule: the core is where the object's idea lands.
6. **Register.** Is the source an object (a rocket) or a form (a stack of plates)? Keep it. Offer, if the person wants, one concept that moves it to the other register.

Then write the concepts as step 1 of `SKILL.md` says, all with the same object and the same parts, differing in the core, the gradient directions and what the hairline says.

## What the card cannot say

- **Light.** A flat source has none. You decide where the ink settles (rule 03) and where the core is (rule 04).
- **Which colour was which.** Colour in the source often encodes parts (red flame, grey body); the piece says it with level instead: the flame is the core, the body fades from it.
- **Whose it is.** A mark that belongs to someone else is theirs to publish or not: say so at hand-over. Never put a company's name or logo inside the drawing (rule 10).

## Without a browser

The card is not written. Read the SVG's own text: each shape's attributes give its box well enough for proportions. Say at hand-over, in these words, that the source was **not seen in a browser**.
